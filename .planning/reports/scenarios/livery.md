# JA8119 in August 1985: livery and shape reference (research only, no repo changes)

## 1. The 1985 JAL livery on 747SR aircraft

**Where this comes from.** I looked at two full left-side photos of JA8119 itself:
- **Haneda, 3 Mar 1985**, Stuart Jessup, CC BY-SA 2.0: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BOEING_747SR-46,_JA8119_,_JAPAN_AIRLINES.jpg
- **Itami, spring 1984**, Harcmac60, CC BY-SA 3.0: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Japan_Airlines_B747SR-46_(JA8119)_at_Itami_Airport_in_1984.jpg

I checked them against written sources. All sizes below are my estimates, scaled from the photos using the main-deck door height (about 1.93 m) as the ruler. They are not published specifications.

| Element | What it looks like | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Upper fuselage | Gloss white from the crown down to the cheatline. The white carries on about 0.2–0.5 m below the cheatline, to roughly the main-deck floor line. | Photos |
| Cheatline, count and colours | Two bands. A thin **red** pinstripe on top (about 0.2 m) sits directly over a wider **navy (紺)** band (about 0.4–0.5 m). ja.wikipedia says the red and navy lines are "placed at the window area" (赤と紺のラインを窓の部分に配し): https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本航空の歴史. A reader comment on a model review says the band is "very dark blue, not black" (secondary source): https://modelairliner.com/classic-crane-japan-air-lines-boeing-747-146-ja8112-by-apollo/ | Photos + cited text |
| Cheatline position | **The cabin windows sit inside the navy band**, and the red stripe runs just above the window tops. The Itami photo shows this clearly. It also explains why the windows almost disappear in the Haneda photo. | Photos |
| Cheatline start and end | It starts at the nose tip, just below the radome centreline, and tapers to a point there. It runs straight along the whole fuselage and ends at the tail cone under the horizontal stabiliser. It **does not sweep up the fin**. Norebbo: "extended the full length of the fuselage": https://www.norebbo.com/japan-airlines-livery/ | Photos + Norebbo |
| Titles | "JAPAN AIR LINES" in **black** capitals (three words), in a bold, wide sans-serif. I could not identify the typeface. The title sits on the **main deck**, just above the red stripe. It runs from just behind door L1, over door L2, to about the wing root. Cap height is about 0.6–0.7 m. The upper deck has no title. | Photos |
| Other markings | A small red disc (the Japanese flag, 日の丸) on white, just behind door L1 at title height. A small, unreadable marking below the cheatline between L1 and L2 (**unverified**). The type name ("BOEING 747") in small white letters inside the navy band, just behind the last door. ja.wikipedia says Boeing aircraft carried it "機体最後部ドアの後ろのラインの紺色部分". | Photos + ja.wikipedia |
| Registration | "JA8119" in small black letters above the cheatline, between doors L4 and L5. I could not see whether it also appeared under the wing (**unverified**). | Photos |
| Fin | White. The red Tsurumaru (鶴丸) is a crane whose wings form a red ring, with white inside the ring and "JAL" in white italic across the bottom of the red. It is centred on the fin chord at about 40–45% of the fin's height above the crown. Its diameter is about 55% of the fin's local chord, roughly 4.5–5 m (photo estimate). ja.wikipedia: 垂直尾翼には大きな赤い「鶴丸」マーク. FOTW says it was designed in 1958 by Jerry Huff: https://www.fotw.info/flags/jp$jal.html | Photos + cited text |
| Lower fuselage | Unpainted aluminium below the white. Norebbo: "The exposed aluminum bottom section was still a predominant part of the livery, but it was reduced in height." In the dusk photo it reads medium grey. | Norebbo + photos |
| Wings and horizontal stabiliser | Light grey (Boeing standard grey). **Unverified from text**, taken from the photos. | Photos |
| Engine nacelles | Metallic grey, about the same tone as the belly, not white. Bright inlet lips and darker exhaust sections. **Unverified from text.** | Photos |

## 4. How the 747SR-46 differs in shape from a 747-400

- **Airframe details.** JA8119 was serial 20783, line 230. Delivery date is 19 Feb 1974 according to https://airport-data.com/aircraft/JA8119.html; ja.wikipedia gives 30 Jan 1974 (the discrepancy is unresolved). It was one of "Seven -100SRs … with … Pratt & Whitney JT9D-7A engines derated to 43,000 pounds-force": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Boeing_747_variants. Airport-data lists **JT9D-7AW** (the water-injection version); this is unresolved but looks the same externally.
- **JT9D nacelles.** Short fan cowl, with an exposed core and a long tapering exhaust.
- **747-400 engines.** "PW4056, … CF6-80C2B1F, and … RB211-524G/H" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400). The nacelle shapes are different.
- **Wings.** The 747-400 spans 211 ft 5 in (64.44 m) and has "6-foot (1.8-metre) tall winglets". The classic 747 spans 195 ft 8 in (59.6 m). So for JA8119, remove the winglets and shorten each wingtip by about 2.4 m. The 747-400D "lacks the wingtip extensions and winglets" (Boeing 747-400 article), so it is geometrically closer to JA8119.
- **Upper deck.** The -300/-400 upper deck is "23-foot-4-inch-longer (7.11 m)", and its stretched upper deck "has two emergency exit doors". The classic had a short upper deck, originally "three windows per side", with "ten windows on either side as an option". Both photos show a row of about 10 upper-deck windows on JA8119 and no upper-deck door. That count is from the photos only; check it at higher resolution.
- **Strengthening.** "Extra structural support was built into the wings, fuselage, and the landing gear along with a 20% reduction in fuel capacity" (List of 747 variants). None of this is visible from outside. Length (70.66 m) and fin shape are essentially the same as the -400.

## 5. What separated at 18:24:35

- **Time.** The Japan Transport Safety Board (JTSB) 2011 explanatory commentary (解説), p.14, gives the time the emergency began: "18 時 24 分 35 秒に異常事態発生": https://jtsb.mlit.go.jp/kaisetsu/nikkou123-kaisetsu.pdf (3.6 MB; index page https://jtsb.mlit.go.jp/kaisetsu/nikkou123.html).
- **Sequence** (p.v): the bulkhead ruptured, then the "APU 防火壁、APU 本体及び尾部胴体構造の一部損壊・脱落" (APU firewall, APU and part of the tail-cone structure damaged and lost), then the vertical fin and hydraulic flight controls were destroyed.
- **Parts lost over Sagami Bay** (Table 5, p.25): the APU (1.9 × 1.4 × 1.0 m, 250 kg), two rudder actuators, the **rearmost fuselage / tail cone (3.4 m long)**, the **fin torsion box** (the main load-carrying box of the fin; 10.2 m high, 900 kg), the **upper rudder** and the **lower rudder**. Pieces of the rudders washed up in Sagami Bay (p.16–17).
- **What stayed on.** Report figure 27 (付図-27, Commons link above) marks which pieces were recovered where. The grid-hatched area recovered at the crash site is the **dorsal fillet and the lower leading edge, forward of the front spar, up to about fin station 395**, plus a strip at the root. The upper leading edge and tip were recovered from the sea. The torsion box and both rudders are almost entirely missing. My estimate from the drawing: **about one-third of the fin's side area stayed on and about two-thirds were lost**. ja.wikipedia agrees (secondary source): "垂直安定板の下半分のみを残して破壊され".
- **Horizontal stabiliser.** It stayed attached. It was found "ほぼ一体" (almost in one piece) about 510 m from the crash point (commentary, p.9 area).
