What I would build

Severity colours, the curtain, the track line and the ahead strip. Clouds stay cloud-shaped and the aircraft disappears in them, but rain and storms carry the colours of the rain radar, so the kind of weather is read at a glance. The hazard area stops being a pink box you sit inside: its edge is a curtain, its ground is hatched, and the status line at the top says whether you are in it.

All three cloud looks run on the same engine, one extra drawing pass over the scene, so the choice between them is only about how it reads. The speed cost is the same for each.

Cloud look

CloudLight rainHeavy rainThunderstorm
Natural
Soft volumes lit by the sun: white fair-weather cloud, grey rain layers, dark storm towers. Closest to the real sky. Severity is read from darkness and shape only.
Severity colours my pick
The same volumes, but rain and storms are tinted like the radar. White means cloud with nothing falling from it.
Blocks
The sky cut into 1 km cells; a cell with weather in it is a solid block in its severity colour. Least like a sky, most like a chart: every edge is exact and nothing is fuzzy.

Hazard area

Curtain my pick
An outline and hatching on the surface, and a curtain along the edge that is strongest at the area's top height. Nothing fills the area, so the view inside stays clean.
Fence
Posts and a top rail in place of the curtain. Lighter still; harder to see far away.
Box (today)
The translucent box the app draws now, for comparison.

With the curtain or the fence, a red frame round the view and the status line say when the aircraft is inside the area and below its top.

Looking ahead

Track line
The next six minutes on this heading and rate of climb, with a tick each minute. It takes the weather's colour where it passes through cloud.
Level slice
A flat cut through the weather at the aircraft's own altitude, with rings at 10, 20 and 40 km. Best read zoomed out, looking down.
Ahead strip
A side view of the next 80 km: what stands in the way, how high it reaches, and when the aircraft gets there.

Zoom

The camera here goes out to 100 km, as the app will. The aircraft never shrinks below a small fixed size on screen and keeps its brackets and flight number, so it stays findable from any distance. The same rule holds for the other aircraft in the app.
